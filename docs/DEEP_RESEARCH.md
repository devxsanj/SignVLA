# Deep research: gaps and novel directions (2026-09-30)

Generated by a multi-agent search with 3-vote claim verification: 25 sources fetched, 25 claims verified, 0 refuted.
Novelty is a narrow gap claim against the papers listed, not a guarantee.

## Summary
The broad claim of being first at sign-to-VLA is taken. SignVLA 2602.22514 (Feb 2026, fingerspelling) and SignVLA 2606.20857 (Jun 2026, hand landmarks, attention-LSTM, alphabet and command signs) both convert signs to text before a downstream VLA. The second paper's recognizer is architecturally close to this project's MediaPipe plus Bi-GRU pipeline, so the recognizer adds no novelty. Gesture conditioning inside VLAs is also crowded: GesVLA 2605.22812, GIVE 2606.13435 and DeicticVLA 2608.28108 all exist. However, all three handle deictic pointing (plus open-palm in GIVE) alongside a text instruction. None uses a symbolic sign vocabulary as the only command channel, whole-hand temporal landmark tokens, or any idle or false-trigger handling. Conformal abstention on its own is not novel either: KnowNo 2307.01928 covers LLM planners and BOKBO 2605.30660 covers K-sample VLA actions. Neither addresses streaming idle rejection on sign or gesture input. The strongest defensible contributions, ranked: (1) an idle-safe sign-command benchmark and method, reporting false commands per hour with streaming risk-controlled or conformal rejection; (2) a small VLA (SmolVLA or TinyVLA style) conditioned directly on temporal whole-hand landmark or sign tokens with no text at all, ablated against the sign-to-text-to-VLA pipeline the two SignVLA papers use. '100% novelty' cannot be guaranteed. The claim should be framed as a narrow, clearly scoped gap measured against named prior work.

## Findings
### (high, vote 3-0 on all merged claims (0, 2, 4))
Being 'first sign-language-driven VLA' is taken, and so is a landmark plus recurrent sign recognizer. SignVLA 2602.22514 claims to be the first sign-driven VLA, using gloss-free, alphabet-level fingerspelling turned into language commands. SignVLA 2606.20857 extracts hand landmarks, uses an attention-enhanced LSTM for alphabet- and command-level signs, and adds a temporal stabilization module, which is close to this project's debounce gate. The project's Bi-GRU recognizer and debounce are therefore not sources of novelty.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22514, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20857

### (high, vote 3-0 (claims 1, 3))
Both SignVLA papers pass signs through a text or language bottleneck before the VLA. A policy conditioned directly on sign or landmark tokens, with no text, is not occupied by either paper.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22514, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20857

### (high, vote 3-0 on claims 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16; 2-1 on claims 8, 10, 15)
Gesture-conditioned VLAs already exist, but every one is deictic and adds gesture to text rather than replacing it. GesVLA feeds a 12-D MediaPipe keypoint vector (wrist plus 3 index-finger joints) from a single keyframe through an MLP into continuous latent tokens. It models no hand shape, no sequence of motion over time, no sign vocabulary and no rejection. GIVE keeps pi0.5's architecture unchanged but fine-tunes all its weights. It draws hand skeletons and fingertip rays onto camera images, and has Gemini write gesture descriptions that are appended to the text prompt. It covers pointing and open-palm for one grasp-then-handover task. DeicticVLA reduces its language, vision-language and visual instruction modes to a text prompt plus deictic masks. So 'non-text hand conditioning of a VLA' is partly occupied. What remains open is narrower: a symbolic sign vocabulary as the only command modality, encoded as temporal whole-hand landmark tokens.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22812, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22812v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13435, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13435v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28108

### (medium, vote 3-0 (claims 22, 23, 24, plus 14 and 16))
Conformal or risk-controlled abstention alone is not a novel contribution. KnowNo (2023) uses conformal prediction for LLM robot planners that ask for help. BOKBO (May 2026) calls itself the first conformal abstention layer for K-sample VLA inference. Neither treats continuous sign or gesture streams, idle-period false triggers, or false commands per hour. The closest thing in gesture-VLA work is GIVE's 1.0 s stability gate, and GIVE reports no false-trigger metric. Streaming, idle-safe rejection with a guarantee for sign-command input appears open, though the method-side literature (open-set gesture spotting, selective conformal risk control) was not fully searched.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30660, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13435v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22812v1

### (high, vote 3-0 (claims 17-21))
A small VLA fits one student's budget, but data is the bottleneck. SmolVLA (2506.01844, about 450M parameters) is designed to train on one GPU and run on consumer GPUs or CPUs, and it conditions only on natural language. TinyVLA (2409.12514) needs no robot-data pre-training: it fine-tunes a small pretrained multimodal backbone and adds a diffusion-policy decoder. That offers a template where a landmark encoder could replace the language input, but this is untested. ROS2SmolVLA (2608.23320) fine-tuned SmolVLA on 349 teleoperated episodes and reached 77.72% pick and 63.59% place success. Place success fell to 46.88% out of distribution, and the authors call the rates too low to deploy because of too little data.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01844, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12514, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23320

### (medium, vote synthesized from the project context and claims 4, 14, 16, 21; not independently verified)
Gaps that stop the project being publishable (Q1), judged against the prior art above: (a) data from one signer, 14 signs plus rest, with no cross-signer or cross-session protocol; the 90% to 78% drop from random to later-recording splits shows a session shift that reviewers will attack; (b) no VLA or learned policy yet, only primitives mapped from a concept layer; (c) no baselines, for example the 2606.20857-style sign-to-text-to-VLA pipeline, GesVLA-style keyframe keypoints, a thresholded classifier, or open-set or spotting methods; (d) no streaming-safety metric, since false commands per hour, idle false-trigger rate, latency to command and risk-coverage curves are all missing, and idle windows misfire as 'back'; (e) no task-success evaluation on the real arm with enough episodes; the ROS2SmolVLA numbers suggest hundreds of demonstrations are needed for manipulation.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20857, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22812v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13435v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23320

### (medium, vote synthesis judgment built on 3-0 claims)
Ranked recommendation. #1, highest defensibility: IDLE-SAFE SIGN COMMANDING. This is a streaming benchmark plus method that controls false commands per hour on continuous hand-landmark streams (long idle, fidgeting, non-command signing), using a conformal or risk-controlled rejection layer calibrated on session-held-out data, with a guarantee on false actuation. Closest prior work: KnowNo 2307.01928, BOKBO 2605.30660, GIVE's 1 s gate (2606.13435), 2606.20857's stabilization module. Novelty verdict: narrow but defensible, because it combines streaming sign input, idle rejection and a guarantee on robot actuation; no verified paper does all three. Minimum experiments: several hours of recorded idle and distractor video; at least 3-5 signers, or a clearly stated single-signer limitation with session-held-out splits; false commands per hour and missed commands at a fixed rate; risk-coverage curves; baselines of softmax threshold, margin, debounce-only, an explicit rest class, energy or OOD scoring, and split-conformal; a check of empirical coverage against the target; a real-arm demo counting unintended motions. Reviewers will attack: exchangeability breaks in streaming data, so the guarantee may not hold; the vocabulary and signer pool are small; the work may be 'just open-set gesture spotting' (cite and compare against 2312.02535 and 2407.19753); and whether the ISL sign vocabulary matters. #2: A TEXT-FREE SIGN-TOKEN SMALL VLA. Feed temporal whole-hand landmark tokens (30x126) directly into a SmolVLA or TinyVLA-style policy in place of the language tokens, and ablate against sign-to-text-to-same-VLA. Closest prior work: GesVLA 2605.22812 (latent tokens, but pointing and 12-D keyframes, text still present) and the two SignVLA papers (text bottleneck). Novelty verdict: moderate, and at risk of being called an incremental encoder swap unless the text-free route shows a measurable benefit, such as lower latency, avoiding compounding errors from the text stage, or passing abstention through to the action level. Minimum experiments: a MuJoCo RoArm task suite plus a subset on the real arm; 50-100+ demonstrations per task; same policy with landmark tokens vs. text tokens vs. oracle text; latency; failure under recognition noise. Reviewers will attack: a 4-DOF arm with 14 commands does not need a VLA; the pretrained VLM language prior is lost; there is too little data; there are no cross-signer results. Recommended combination: make #1 the main paper and #2 an extension or workshop paper, possibly joined by carrying abstention through to the action level.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30660, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22812, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20857, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22514, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01844, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12514

## Caveats
1. '100% novelty' cannot be guaranteed. The novelty verdicts are narrow gap claims measured against the papers found, and a concurrent or unindexed preprint could take either direction at any time; this area moved monthly through 2026 (Feb, May, June and Aug papers).
2. Most checks read abstracts only. Full text was read only for GesVLA, GIVE and ROS2SmolVLA, so the SignVLA papers could contain ablations or rejection mechanisms not seen here.
3. Several claims rest on a paper's own 'first' statements (2602.22514, BOKBO), which are self-reported.
4. The method-side prior art for idle rejection was only flagged by a verifier, not verified: open-set gesture spotting (2312.02535, 2407.19753) and Selective Conformal Risk Control (2512.12844).
5. No surviving claims covered Octo, MiniVLA, pi0-small, sign-grounded action chunking, or cross-sign-language concept grounding, so those directions are unassessed, not cleared.
6. The ISL-specific literature and non-arXiv venues (IEEE, ACM, Indian conferences) were not searched systematically.
7. The Q1 gap list and the ranking are synthesis judgments, not verified facts.
8. Feasibility claims for Mac/MPS training and for the 4-DOF RoArm are inferences.
9. The split votes (2-1) concern how to characterize GIVE's and DeicticVLA's pathways; they do not affect the landmark-token conclusion.
10. Dates are as of 2026-09-30.

## Open questions
- Does published open-set or streaming gesture-spotting work already report false-positives-per-hour with conformal guarantees in HRI or robot settings, which would shrink recommendation #1 to 'applied to ISL plus a robot arm'?
- Can conformal guarantees be made valid under the temporal dependence of streaming windows (for example with online or adaptive conformal methods), and how much does this change false commands per hour in practice?
- Does conditioning a small VLA directly on temporal landmark tokens give a measurable benefit (latency, robustness to recognition noise, fewer compounding errors) over sign-to-text-to-VLA, or is it only an encoder swap?
- Is there prior work on cross-sign-language (ISL/ASL/BSL) concept grounding to shared robot primitives that would affect using the language-agnostic concept layer as a contribution?
